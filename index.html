Note: Symptom response to an empirical PPI trial may guide initial symptom control, but is not sensitive or specific enough to confirm GERD. Alarm symptoms require clinical assessment.
What are alarm symptoms?

Alarm symptoms may be indicative of other conditions that warrant further diagnostic workup, such as oesophageal strictures or oesophageal adenocarcinoma:

  1. Dysphagia (difficult swallowing)
  2. Odynophagia (painful swallowing)
  3. Unexplained weight loss
  4. Gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding
  5. Vomiting
  6. Anaemia

→

No

Twice-daily PPI

Continue for 8–12 weeks, with appropriate administration.

→
Reassess after 8–12 weeks

Has treatment produced an adequate response?

Yes
Individualise maintenance

Use the lowest effective PPI dose and schedule when ongoing therapy is indicated.

No
Investigate refractory symptoms

Further workup may include endoscopy and reflux testing.

If endoscopy shows erosive oesophagitis (EE)
  • Treat with a PPI for 8 weeks, at a standard dose or up to twice daily as clinically indicated.
  • For Los Angeles (LA) grade C or D, long-term maintenance PPI or anti-reflux surgery may be needed.
  • Use the lowest effective dose for maintenance.
Important: avoid unnecessary dose escalation
  • Confirm adherence and correct PPI administration.
  • Review whether the symptoms are acid-related.
  • Consider alarm symptoms, complications and alternative diagnoses.

3. Maintenance GERD therapy

A. Who needs maintenance PPI?

Patients with GERD complications

Maintenance treatment helps prevent recurrence and complications.

→
Typical indications
  • Severe EE (LA grade C or D)
  • Barrett’s oesophagus (BE)
/
Without EE or BE

When symptoms recur after stopping PPIs, on-demand therapy may be suitable.

B. Treatment of refractory symptoms

Principle: Do not routinely add medicines to PPIs. Choose an adjunct according to the symptom pattern and evidence of reflux.
Nocturnal symptomsBedtime H₂ receptor antagonist

May help selected patients with nighttime symptoms.

Caution: Tachyphylaxis can develop with continuous use.

After mealsAlginic acid

May reduce postprandial breakthrough reflux symptoms.

Regurgitation and belchingBaclofen

May help selected patients; adverse effects and limited evidence restrict routine use.

C. On-demand PPI use

Take PPI when symptoms recur

Continue during symptomatic periods, then stop after symptoms resolve.

→
Best candidates: non-erosive reflux disease (NERD)

Patients without complications requiring continuous therapy.

→
Do not replace required maintenance

Not appropriate for BE or severe EE requiring long-term acid suppression.

Potential benefits: On-demand use may reduce total PPI exposure by approximately 60–80%. More than 80% of patients were willing to continue this approach.

D. Deprescribing considerations

Review PPI use after 8–12 weeks

Does the patient still need long-term acid suppression?

→
Clear indication to continue?

Assess complications and risk factors before deprescribing.

→
Yes: maintain when indicated

Continue at the lowest effective dose and appropriate schedule.

Examples of indications for ongoing therapy
  • BE
  • Severe EE (LA grade C or D)
  • Oesophageal ulcer
  • Peptic stricture
  • Eosinophilic oesophagitis, when prescribed as treatment
  • High risk of upper GI bleeding

Important qualification: Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis alone is not a reason to prescribe a PPI solely for respiratory benefit.

No ongoing indication: consider stopping

Discuss rebound acid hypersecretion (RAHS). It may occur after approximately 2 months of continuous PPI treatment.

If severe symptoms persist for more than 2 months after stopping, reassess for ongoing GERD or a non-acid cause.

Discontinuation strategy

Either gradual dose tapering or abrupt discontinuation may be reasonable. Tailor the approach to the patient.

→
Manage breakthrough symptoms

Use on-demand PPIs, H₂ receptor antagonists or antacids when appropriate.

Quick summary

Start with the least intensive therapy for mild symptoms. Escalate according to frequency and response, use the lowest effective PPI dose, and regularly reassess the need for long-term treatment.
